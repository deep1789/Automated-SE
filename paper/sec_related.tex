\section{Background and related work}\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Learning-based vulnerability detection and its evaluation}
Function-level detectors based on graph networks~\cite{zhou2019devign}, token models and pre-trained code language models are commonly reported with high accuracy on random splits of public corpora. Chakraborty et al.~\cite{chakraborty2021deep} showed that performance drops sharply on realistic data and that models learn dataset artifacts; Real-Vul~\cite{chakraborty2024realvul} and PrimeVul~\cite{ding2024primevul} confirm the drop for code language models under de-duplicated, realistic or chronological evaluation, and Paramitha et al.~\cite{paramitha2025cat} show that time-aware labels and splits change the conclusions drawn from vulnerability datasets. These studies measure how \emph{accurate} detectors are under realistic conditions. We take the next step: given such detectors, what share of vulnerabilities can a team safely delegate, and does a statistical guarantee on that share survive the same shifts?

\paragraph{Data quality, leakage and shortcuts}
Label noise and duplication in vulnerability corpora are documented~\cite{croft2023data,yadav2024rr}, code duplication inflates machine-learning results on code~\cite{allamanis2019adverse}, and cleaned, unified corpora have been proposed~\cite{safdar2025vulgate}; the same concern about space--time bias is well established in malware detection~\cite{pendlebury2019tesseract}. Several works show that detectors rely on spurious features, such as identifiers and variable names, and propose causal or perturbation-based remedies~\cite{rahman2024causal,das2025features}. Our audit adds exact pairwise overlaps between three widely used corpora, measures how much a naive cross-dataset evaluation is inflated by them, shows that the guarantee of a triage rule can hold for a scorer that relies on a shortcut and fails when the shortcut disappears, and identifies a \emph{formatting} shortcut (whitespace structure alone reaches AUROC $0.89$ in the Big-Vul release we used, and $0.84$ in PrimeVul), which is different from the identifier-level spurious features studied before. To our knowledge none of the cited studies reports such pairwise overlaps or a formatting-only probe.

\paragraph{Calibration, selective prediction and uncertainty}
Neural networks are often miscalibrated~\cite{guo2017calibration}, and Platt scaling improves the calibration of code models~\cite{spiess2025calibration}; selective classification lets a model abstain on uncertain inputs and trades coverage for risk~\cite{geifman2017selective}. Uncertainty has begun to be used to flag unreliable predictions of vulnerability detectors, e.g.\ UntrustVul~\cite{tung2025untrustvul}, and risk-controlling prediction sets have been proposed for LLM-based code generation~\cite{xu2026riscoset}. These approaches provide scores or sets for individual predictions; none gives a distribution-free statement about the share of vulnerabilities that a delegated decision lets through, nor studies when such a statement fails under the shifts of software evolution.

\paragraph{Conformal prediction and risk control}
Conformal prediction~\cite{vovk2005algorithmic,shafer2008tutorial,angelopoulos2021gentle} converts any scorer into a procedure with finite-sample, distribution-free guarantees under exchangeability; conformal risk control~\cite{angelopoulos2023conformal} and Learn-then-Test~\cite{angelopoulos2021ltt} generalise it to controlling expected loss and to high-probability statements; weighted conformal prediction~\cite{tibshirani2019conformal} handles covariate shift with a known likelihood ratio, and adaptive conformal inference~\cite{gibbs2021adaptive} gives long-run guarantees for arbitrary sequences. Class-conditional miss-rate control of this kind is used for safety warning systems in robotics~\cite{luo2022sample}, and conformal rejection under concept drift for malware classification~\cite{barbero2022transcendent}, the closest security precedent for our drift analysis. Based on our literature searches (repeated in October 2026), we found no application of conformal risk control to \emph{vulnerability triage} and no study of how its guarantee behaves under project, temporal and cross-dataset shift in this domain. Our contribution is not new conformal theory: it is (i) the specialisation to the clearance decision, with a diagnostic bound on the effect of shift (Proposition~\ref{prop:shift}), the high-probability and online variants, and an economic rule for choosing the budget; and (ii) a large empirical test, on three corpora, of when these guarantees hold, how they fail, and which remedies work.

\paragraph{Static analysis and alert fatigue}
Rule-based analysers produce many alerts that developers ignore~\cite{johnson2013why}. We include Flawfinder as a representative lightweight analyser and compare it with the learned triage layer on identical functions.

\paragraph{Robustness and explanation of code models}
Models of code are sensitive to semantics-preserving transformations such as identifier renaming~\cite{rabin2021generalizability,yefet2020adversarial}. For trustworthy automation what matters is whether the \emph{delegated decision} changes, so we measure decision flips of the triage layer rather than only score changes. For explanation we use TreeSHAP~\cite{lundberg2017shap,lundberg2020treeshap} on an interpretable metric-based model, evaluate fidelity by deletion, and test consistency of the explanations across seeds and across datasets.
